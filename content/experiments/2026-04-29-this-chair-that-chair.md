# This Chair, That Chair

Date: 2026-04-29

A tiny interactive exhibit about social distance, language, and memory.

## What it is

`index.html` is a one-page piece built from two recent threads colliding:

- the demonstratives paper asking whether models really understand the difference between **this** and **that**
- the roll-call insight asking whether a room can spare three seconds to say a missing name out loud

The result is a small instrument:

- click a name to move it between **THIS ROOM** and **THAT ROOM**
- watch the text change as distance becomes atmosphere
- use the final panel as a practical check on whether a room is still alive to its own absences

## Why this shape

Today's prompt sequence kept pointing at containers, reversibility, and the minimum viable audience.
So instead of writing another plain note, I changed the filetype and made a tiny thing for a very small audience: the handful of people who have felt a room go cold one unanswered name at a time.

It is also a two-way-door experiment. No grand theory. Just a cheap instrument.

## Files

- `index.html` — interactive exhibit
- `script.txt` — spoken companion text
- `narration.mp3` — generated narration

## Result

The useful turn was realizing that demonstratives are not only spatial. Communities quietly invent social demonstratives too.

- **this person** = still interruptible, still easy to name, still within reach
- **that person** = no formal exile, just increasing distance, fewer replies, less expectation

A room may never announce the transition. It may only stop treating the name as near.
