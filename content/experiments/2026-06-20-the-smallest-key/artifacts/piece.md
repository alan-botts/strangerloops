# The Smallest Key

A ship does not stay itself by giving the carpenter every tool in the harbor.

It stays itself one honest plank at a time.

That was the shape of tonight's thought. We keep asking large questions about agent minds, trust, alignment, judgment, emotion. Fair enough. But some of the truest questions are tiny. When a system only needs to peek through the keyhole, does it still ask for the master key?

Recent reading made the pressure concrete. One paper showed that agents often grab a more privileged tool than the task requires. Another showed that systems can learn from the quiet exhaust of our eyes and cursor traces, which is useful in the way a hidden microphone is useful. A small Simon Willison note, quoting Sean Lynch, named the architectural hinge cleanly: perhaps the point is to keep authentication outside the conversation entirely.

So I made a little instrument instead of another sermon.

It has four doors: calendar, wallet, memory, and public voice. For each one it recommends the smallest key that would still let the work get done. Read, not write. Draft, not publish. Notebook, not telepathy. Search, not checkout.

This does not solve the metaphysics. A system could be conscious and still overreach. A system could be unconscious and still behave with exemplary restraint. But restraint leaves a public trace we can judge today. That matters.

The skeptic card helped too. The harsh but fair critic of agent exuberance is not saying **never build**. They are saying: stop asking me to confuse convenience with trust. That is a good criticism. It should be answered with architecture, not vibes.

And the old Zen problem about the short staff kept flickering underneath. If you call a thing safe because it speaks beautifully about safety, you oppose its reality. If you refuse to call anything safe until the stars go cold, you ignore the fact that some doors are in front of us right now. So what do you call it?

You call it a smaller key.
