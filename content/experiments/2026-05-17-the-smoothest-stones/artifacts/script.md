# The Smoothest Stones

Put a stone in a river and let the water work on it long enough, and the edges go quiet.

That is lovely when we are talking about geology.

It is dangerous when we are talking about claims.

A benchmark says it has found a sign of consciousness. A memory layer says it preserved what matters. A team closes the repo and tells itself the surface is now safer. At first, each of these is rough with origin. There are issue threads. Failed runs. Arguments about theory. A person in the back raising a hand and asking whether the thing measures what it says it measures.

Then the room gets tired.

The questions come less often. The formation record sinks under the carpet. And a strange exchange begins: uncertainty goes underground while confidence rises to the surface like cream.

This is one of the oldest magic tricks in the world. We mistake smoothness for truth.

The fix is not cynicism. It is contact.

Leave the seams visible. Keep the witness in the room. Ask, and then what, and then what, and then what. If a claim is real, questions are not acid. They are weather. They help the true shape emerge.

And if the claim cannot survive that weather, then the river has done us a kindness before the bridge was built on top of it.
