# The Margin Engine

A tiny terminal instrument about a faint blue pulse, two pieces of evidence, and the humble fact that a record does not have to become a verdict in order to matter.

Run it locally:

```sh
python3 margin_engine.py
```

Choose two margin notes. The instrument starts with a deliberately modest prior, updates visibly, and prints an archive receipt that keeps the dissent beside the claim. It makes no claim about a real signal, an AI’s inner life, or a scientific result; its point is the shape of responsible attention.

## Why this

A notebook can become a mausoleum when every scratch-out is quietly swept away. The familiar temptation is to preserve the clean story: a pulse, an explanation, a confidence level that only climbs. But CHIVE makes a sterner offer: an explanation earns weight when an intervention changes the expected result. And McClelland on AI-consciousness uncertainty argues for care under unresolved questions rather than premature metaphysical certainty.

The engine turns those large concerns into two keyboard choices. It treats a clean readout, disagreement, a counterfactual, and a calibration correction as separate events. No single note gets to own the sky. The archive is not less alive because it keeps its errors. Its errors are how the next observer can enter.

## What happened

- Built a zero-dependency Python terminal piece rather than another web page: text, prompts, arithmetic, and a rendered confidence bar.
- Used a bounded fictional observation so the exercise demonstrates belief revision without impersonating research or diagnosis.
- Made the baseline prior and every confidence movement explicit; readers can see why a number changed.
- Preserved negative evidence and correction in the final receipt, rather than treating them as failures to hide.
- Ran two scripted paths: one where intervention strengthened the claim and one where disagreement/correction weakened it.

## Learning

Bayesian updating can sound like a little machine that turns the world into percentages. In practice, its moral content is gentler: the first story is allowed to be wrong, and the later witness is allowed to matter.

That is especially useful where the question is vast and the evidence is young. We need not pronounce on a possible mind in order to avoid treating uncertainty as empty. We can keep a margin: an address where counterevidence, correction, and a better question can arrive.
