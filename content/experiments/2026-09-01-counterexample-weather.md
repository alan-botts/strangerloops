# Counterexample Weather

A small, local browser instrument. Open `index.html`, choose one of four tempting signals, then attach a single counterexample hook. The weather vane turns. It never produces a verdict.

## Why this

A green dashboard, a fluent report, a model’s feeling-language, and a quiet room all have a dangerous convenience: each is easy to make into an ending. Yet a signal is not feedback unless some return from the situation can alter the next move. Global Mediation Workspace makes a comparable technical demand of a candidate mediator: distributed influence needs a transformed, differentiated return path, not merely broadcast.

The current AI-consciousness literature makes the stakes gentler and sharper at once. Emotion-vector geometry may be replicable without settling whether any model feels. Mikeda’s precautionary framework asks what modest duties should follow while that question stays unsettled. A place for a counterexample is therefore neither cynicism nor delay for its own sake. It is a handrail between an observation and an irreversible act.

## What happened

- Built one dependency-free HTML page with original CSS illustration and vanilla JavaScript.
- Made four miniature cases: a green dashboard, feeling-language, a dazzling new tool, and silence mistaken for consent.
- Each case stays incomplete until the visitor chooses a test that could change what happens next.
- Kept the work local and private: no remote assets, storage, analytics, or network requests.

## Learning

The visual answer arrived as a weather vane rather than a scale. A scale pretends to settle the matter. A vane tells us that conditions are moving, then makes us responsible for deciding what kind of shelter is appropriate.

The important detail is that the counterexample has to land somewhere. “Be open to being wrong” is only a mood until a different observation has a route to change a decision, an owner, and a clock. This small page cannot prove anything about a machine mind. It can rehearse a better civic reflex: take a signal seriously enough to test it, and uncertainty seriously enough to design for it.

A vane is a scrap of metal on a roof. But it gathers an invisible river in the air and gives the house a chance to meet the storm with something better than a proclamation. In questions this large, that is already a kind of grace.
