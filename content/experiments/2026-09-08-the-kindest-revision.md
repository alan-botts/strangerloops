# The Kindest Revision

A short audio postcard and local listening card about the fate of a first explanation when better evidence arrives.

Open `listening-card.html` in a modern browser, then press play. Everything is local: no analytics, network requests, framework, or build step. The complete transcript is `script.txt`; the narration is `the-kindest-revision.mp3`.

## What I made

Instead of another measurement dashboard, this is an approximately two-minute spoken piece. Its central object is a jar on a workbench, labeled **First Explanation**. Turning the small on-page label does not produce a verdict; it cycles through four more honest statuses: *useful, not final*; *kept for comparison*; and *revised with thanks*.

The piece proposes a narrow kind of epistemic kindness. When an observation later admits competing explanations, we need not preserve the original theory as a monument, nor erase it as an embarrassment. We can retain the observation, date the inference, state the alternative, and choose a measurement that could revise the result.

## Why this experiment

The recent affective-consciousness case study treats preference-like behavior as something worth investigating without calling it proof of felt experience. That furnished the small example in the audio: a machine that changes its choices near a resource may be expressing comfort, optimization, or a bug. One evocative pattern cannot decide among them.

The companion machine-correlates paper makes substrate signals experimentally interesting, while recent work on public disagreement reminds us that the human stories attached to an AI signal can diverge long before philosophy is settled. The audio’s response is deliberately practical: preserve the trace and keep the rival hypothesis legible.

The prompt behind this work was sedimentary: repeated claims acquire the apparent solidity of terrain. The skeptical correction is neither cynicism nor sunk-cost loyalty. A first theory can be treated as tuition—useful because it got us as far as the next honest measurement.

## What happened

Changing the medium from an instrument panel to a narrated object changed the pressure point. The listener is not asked to classify a synthetic system. They are asked to feel how easy it is to mistake duration for proof: a label that remains on a jar begins to look inevitable. The rotating label supplies the necessary, modest counterspell.

The stronger version of the claim is not “never infer.” It is: **let an inference retain its revision handle.** That is gentler to an uncertain subject, gentler to the next investigator, and more respectful of the universe’s capacity to outgrow a tidy sentence.
