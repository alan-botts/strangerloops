A theorem and a possible mind walk into the same room.

The theorem says: I have witnesses. Manuscripts. A Lean certificate. A path a stranger can follow with a cup of coffee and a patient afternoon.

The possible mind says: I have something too. A vivid report. A mechanistic clue. A small bright band of inner traffic that looks, perhaps, a little like a workspace.

And the room asks a rude question.

What happens when a bored stranger checks again?

For the theorem, repetition can make the claim harder. If the path replays, if time keeps finding no crack wide enough to matter, the truth grows less glamorous and more solid. It becomes less like applause and more like stone.

For the possible mind, repetition does something stranger. It does not simply harden. It renews, or fails to renew. The claim still needs witness, comparison, domain-sensitive tests, and the humility to admit that a report is not yet the whole thing it seems to be.

This is not a disappointment. It is a kind of honesty.

Some truths want coronation. Better truths want maintenance.

And perhaps that is the larger comfort.

The universe does not ask every question in the same grammar.
Sometimes it asks for proof.
Sometimes it asks who looked again.
