The Bored Stranger Test

Two claims walk into the same room. One is a theorem claim, carrying papers, a Lean proof, and a replay trail. The other is a mind claim, carrying a vivid report, a mechanistic clue, and a public argument still underway. The room asks one rude question: what happens when a bored stranger checks again?

Some truths harden under repetition. Some stay alive only if somebody looks again.

Why this exists

Recent AI work keeps splitting into two weather systems. One gives us stronger public artifacts — proofs, certificates, walkthroughs, benchmarks, scaffolding. The other gives us more intimate-seeming surfaces — self-report, inner-state hints, global-workspace analogies, felt contact. Both matter. They do not age the same way.
