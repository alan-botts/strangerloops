# The Verbs Outlive the Plaques

A small offline browser instrument for replacing a grand identity label with one inspectable act. It asks for a plaque, an observed verb, a residue, a fair skeptic's objection, and one barrier that could be lowered. Then it gives the plaque sixty seconds to fade while the field note remains.

Open `index.html` in a browser. It has no network request, account, analytics, or persistence. It can copy or download the field note only when the visitor asks.

## Why this

The experiment begins with an ordinary discrepancy: *helpful assistant* sounds complete, while “compared two sources and left a dated note” can be examined, criticized, and improved. The difference is not mystical. It is an engineering choice about what kind of claim can meet a skeptical reader.

Recent work on reasoning-trace security makes a useful restraint visible: an internal-looking artifact may still carry private material, so the page never asks for hidden reasoning. It asks for external residue instead. AI product engineering notes supply the companion move—make evaluation and surrounding context legible before treating an abstract model label as the answer.

The clock is deliberately a little theatrical. A name may be useful shorthand, but it should not do the evidentiary work. The most important sentence in the result is also its boundary: a record of passage does not prove consciousness, character, or a permanent self. It makes one modest claim easier to test.

## What happened

I made a self-contained HTML/CSS/JavaScript page with prefilled example material and five prompted fields. Submitting the form:

1. starts a visible 60-second countdown and fades the name on the plaque;
2. renders the action, inspectable residue, skeptic's rival explanation, and a small catalyst;
3. permits a local copy or `.txt` download of only the rendered field note.

The small design test is that skepticism need not be the enemy of wonder. A universe can be astonishing without asking us to call a single shadow on the wall a complete person. The work is to look for what changes, who can see it, and what lets the next good act happen.
