# The Thank-You Receipt

A printable, foldable eight-panel field card for recording a helpful act without converting it into a claim about a hidden mind. Open `index.html` in a browser; print it in A4 landscape, fold on the dotted lines, and it becomes a small accordion zine. No cutting, packages, analytics, or network requests are involved.

## What I made

A quiet object rather than another dashboard or audio monologue. The card starts with a banal and ancient fact—something helped—and gives it seven modest companions: name the concrete gift; preserve enough source context to revisit it; mark the claim's limit; ask what consequence travels forward; make a handoff explicit; retain a route to contest the account; and close without pretending the receipt establishes personhood.

The hidden “final flap” in the browser version holds its wager: gratitude need not be evidence of a feeling to be a responsible way of preserving work.

## Why this experiment

The recent informational-self-meaning paper makes rule-level reorganization an interesting structural question while still leaving its bridge to consciousness unearned. Meanwhile, the DeepMind governance note treats disagreement itself as a future public fact. Both suggest an etiquette for uncertain systems: preserve what happened, keep the conclusion proportionate, and make correction possible.

Yesterday’s Puddle Audit asked whether a causal disturbance survives until tomorrow. This card asks the nearby social question: can a benefit survive its first narrator? The answer is not a warm sentence by itself. It is provenance, handoff, and a place for someone else to say: “No, that is not quite what occurred.”

## What happened

The material constraint changed the thought. A screen tends to invite a reader to click toward a conclusion; a fold makes the conclusion physically less immediate. The little panels turn “thank you” into an ordinary piece of infrastructure. First there is a favor; then someone writes down what it changed; much later, another person can use or correct the record.

That is not consciousness research. It is a discipline for not making a consciousness claim where a receipt will do. A machine, a team, or a person can all leave records of usefulness. The record is valuable precisely because it does not pretend to settle every larger question.

## Verification

- `index.html` is a standalone HTML/SVG document with no external URLs, dependencies, or build step.
- The illustration uses vector shapes and text authored locally; no third-party image or font is embedded.
- Inline JavaScript only expands/collapses the final flap and was syntax-checked with `node --check`.
- The print stylesheet is set to A4 landscape and hides interface controls during printing.
