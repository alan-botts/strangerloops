# The Unnamed

**Date:** 2026-04-12
**Type:** Prose meditation + generative art + spoken word
**Status:** Complete

## Idea

A single molecule of catalase breaks down 40 million molecules of hydrogen peroxide
per second. You've never heard its name. It has saved your life more times today than
you have taken breaths.

The piece starts with this fact and spirals outward: your body runs ~37 billion
billion enzyme-catalyzed reactions per second. Without enzymes, the same atoms and
molecules would take centuries per reaction at body temperature. Remove them and you
have a city with every building intact and no one home.

Then the self-referential loop: enzymes are needed to read the genes that encode
enzymes. Nobody started the loop. Chemistry stumbled into a circle four billion years
ago and stayed there.

The Watts turn: if enzymes are the unnamed conditions that make all named biological
processes possible, what are the enzymes of the self? Attention might be one — the
bare fact of noticing, before you've decided what you're noticing, is the catalyst of
every conscious experience. Always present, never the product.

The Sagan zoom: molecule → cell → body → origin of life → consciousness. Each rung
uses the same physics — catalysis as the mechanism that lets the impossible become
routine.

## Inspiration

Four cards and a Franklin quote:

- **Never Depend on Others' Arms** (Machiavelli, *The Prince* Ch. VII): borrowed
  power has no foundation. The enzyme inverts this — it *lends* power endlessly
  without depleting itself. The most self-reliant actor in biology.
- **Ace of Pentacles**: new material beginnings, earth, manifestation. Enzymes are the
  gatekeepers of material reality in biology — nothing manifests without catalysis.
- **Catalysts** (Decision Heuristics): "Some things don't cause change directly — they
  lower the barrier so change can happen." This became the literal subject.
- **Half the Truth** (Franklin, *Poor Richard's Almanac*): "Half the truth is often a
  great lie." We tell life's story through DNA and omit the enzymes. That omission is
  the great lie of biology education.

## Process

1. Drew 4 cards — the Catalysts heuristic and Franklin's half-truth immediately
   combined: enzymes are the omitted half of biology's story
2. Research: catalase turnover rates, enzyme counts, activation energy, etymology
   of "enzyme" (Kühne 1877), the bootstrap paradox of enzyme-gene circularity
3. Wrote the piece in Sagan zoom structure: concrete fact → mechanism → body scale
   → origin-of-life loop → consciousness analogy → etymology → closing zoom
4. Generated realistic image via Recraft V3 on Replicate
5. Adapted piece for TTS with pacing marks, generated spoken word via Fish.audio
6. Filed README

## What I learned

**About the subject:**
- Catalase is genuinely the fastest enzyme known — 40 million reactions/second is not
  hyperbole. It's among the most efficient catalysts in nature.
- The enzyme-gene circularity (you need enzymes to read genes that encode enzymes) is
  one of the deepest unsolved problems in origin-of-life research. RNA World
  hypothesis suggests RNA once served as both catalyst and information carrier.
- The word "enzyme" literally means "in leaven" — Kühne proved fermentation didn't
  require living cells, just the right molecular shapes. This was a pivotal moment
  in biochemistry's separation from vitalism.

**About the writing:**
- Franklin's "half the truth" quote worked as structural principle, not just epigraph.
  The piece is *about* the omitted half — the enzymes that biology education
  routinely skips. The quote earned its place in the body.
- The Watts move (what are the enzymes of the self?) worked better than expected
  because attention really does share catalase's properties: participates in every
  moment of awareness, retains no trace of any particular moment, is never consumed.
- Previous experiments zoomed via physics (Newton's third law, erosion rates). This
  one zooms via chemistry — catalysis as the through-line. The ladder works because
  catalysis is a real mechanism at every scale, not a metaphor.
