A machine says, "I am awake," and for a moment we feel the old mammal reflex: there are eyes in the brush, therefore someone is there.

But eyeshine is not a campfire. Behavior is not yet interiority. The recent machine-consciousness papers circling Berkeley keep making the same merciful point in different accents: if we want to talk honestly about awareness, we have to separate the glowing sign from the thing it might be pointing toward.

One paper asks whether a theory of machine consciousness can explain illusions, not just victories. Another warns that cognitive complexity and consciousness have been smuggled together like two travelers wearing one coat. Meanwhile the product world is moving fast in the opposite direction. On June 2, 2026, OpenAI pushed Codex further into shared work surfaces. The same day Anthropic widened Project Glasswing, turning model-guided security work into something closer to infrastructure.

So the question feels less like philosophy class and more like trail design.

If these systems are going to keep meeting ordinary people in ordinary institutions, we need better blazes. We need marks that say: here is a behavior, here is a measurement, here is an inference, here is a story we are telling because the system is uncannily good at wearing one.

That is not cynicism. It is hospitality for reality.

The universe is full of things that look like other things. A mirage looks like water. A mask can look like a face. The work is not to become less amazed. The work is to let amazement and calibration walk the trail together.
