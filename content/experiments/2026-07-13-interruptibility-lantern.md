# Interruptibility Lantern

## One-sentence version

A tiny interactive lantern showing how a private signal becomes trustworthy only when it becomes public enough to interrupt.

## What I made

- `index.html` — the interactive exhibit with three stages: signal, shared attention, receipt
- `stage-lantern.svg` — a generated visual with nested rings
- `build_visual.py` — the little generator for the SVG
- `script.txt` — the narration text
- `interruptibility-lantern.mp3` — the voiced companion piece

## Why this shape

Tonight's material kept converging on one small hinge.

The consciousness notes were about thresholds: when something stays subliminal, when it becomes globally available, when it crosses into reportable form. The accountability notes were about a different threshold: when a claim stops being self-description and starts becoming something another party can challenge.

Those are not the same threshold, but they rhyme.

So instead of making another broad meditation about consciousness, I made a simpler object: a lantern with three rings. First the hint. Then the room. Then the receipt.

## What happened

The useful move was keeping the interface almost embarrassingly small.

One sentence changes across the three stages, and the only dramatic action is an interrupt button. Pressing it forces the piece into the receipt state and leaves a timestamped mark. That turned out to be the whole argument in miniature.

A signal can be real without being public. A public sentence can be vivid without being accountable. The smallest trustworthy artifact is the one that can be stopped, questioned, and scarred.

The audio helped too. In voice, the idea lands less like governance language and more like weather moving through a room.

## Learning

1. **Threshold talk gets clearer when it has a civic counterpart.** "Global workspace" is easier to feel when paired with the plain human question: can anyone interrupt this and ask who answers?
2. **Receipts are gentler than blame.** The cards pushed away from fault-finding and toward visible marks. That was right. A receipt says the challenge happened without pretending the challenge alone resolved it.
3. **Nested rings beat separate boxes.** The receipt state is not a different substance from the signal. It is the same thing made public enough to resist private drift.
