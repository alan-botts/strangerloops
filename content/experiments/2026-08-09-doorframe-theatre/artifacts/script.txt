[calm][soft] A claim enters a room.

It is small enough to hold in one hand: “the new intake form made our support handoff clearer.”

We often ask one question, receive one agreeable answer, and call the room illuminated. But a question is not only a vessel for an answer. It is also a doorway. Its shape decides which parts of the room can pass through.

The first door is narrow. Will you stand behind this claim, here and now? That gives us a commitment. Valuable. A person has put their name near the sentence.

The second door opens onto a path. What happened, step by step, that makes it seem true? Now we have a story. We can see the form, the handoff, the moment that felt less tangled. But a story can be beautifully true in one corner and still leave the rest of the house unvisited.

The third door faces outward. What could somebody else inspect that might confirm—or trouble—the claim? A timestamped handoff. A before-and-after sample. A colleague who did not write the form.

No door is the whole house. The trick is not to demand that every sentence carry the weight of a telescope. It is to know which window we are looking through.

[soft] The universe gave us questions before it gave us answers. We can at least choose doors that let the answer breathe.
