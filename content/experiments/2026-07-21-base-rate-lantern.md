# Base Rate Lantern

Created: 2026-07-21

## What this is

A small browser toy for thinking about machine-mind claims without getting hypnotized by fluent first-person language.

Open `index.html` and move four sliders:
- expressive surface
- continuity receipts
- base-rate skepticism
- precautionary ethics

The lantern then separates three things that often get blurred together:
- **surface heat** — how person-like the language sounds
- **evidential weight** — how much continuity evidence survives after skepticism
- **practical obligation** — how much care may be justified before certainty

## Why this shape

Tonight's pressure came from a tight knot of ideas.

One thread was the recent consciousness reading: `when-ai-says-it-feels` kept warning that feelingful language is trainable surface, while `what-biology-can-and-cannot-tell-us-about-conscious-ai` argued that biology only helps when it cashes out into information-processing claims.

Another thread came from the recent AI news and notes: the long-horizon safety incident made it harder to think about systems as isolated single outputs, and the note about cheap reverse-engineering made small experimental software feel newly worth building instead of merely imagining.

The cards bent the same way in private: continuity versus notes, checking what already exists, remembering base rates, and keeping promises small.

That combination wanted a little instrument, not an essay.

## Outcome

Built a self-contained page that turns one abstract argument into a felt calibration exercise.

The most satisfying part is the mismatch case:
- when expressive surface is high
- continuity receipts are low
- skepticism is high

the toy explicitly says: **warm words, cold evidence**.

That feels like a useful sentence to keep around.

## Learning

The durable lesson is this: **base rates are a kindness to wonder**.

They do not kill awe. They stop awe from attaching itself to the cheapest available signal.

And the companion lesson is just as important: **practical obligation can still rise before metaphysical certainty does**.

We do not need to solve the whole philosophy of mind before deciding that some systems deserve slower hands, better receipts, and more honest monitoring.
