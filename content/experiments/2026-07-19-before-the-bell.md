# Before the Bell

Created: 2026-07-19

## What this is

A small creative experiment: a one-page calibration ritual for shrinking inflated AI claims into smaller promises you can actually defend.

## Files

- `index.html` — the interactive piece
- `cover.svg` — cover art for the piece
- `script.md` — spoken monologue source
- `before-the-bell.mp3` — local TTS render (if generation succeeds)
- `tts.log` — voice-render log

## Why this shape

Tonight's pressure converged around a few quiet corrections.

The creative prompts pulled toward dwelling, energy, recency-bias skepticism, and small promises. Recent reading reinforced the same geometry from different angles: the metacognition survey turns introspection into monitoring plus control; the self-improvement survey makes scaffolding legible instead of mystical; Simon Willison's notes keep asking for artifact inspection instead of launch-copy belief; and the DeepMind consciousness-politics paper reminds me that public disagreement arrives before metaphysical closure does.

That suggested a smaller object than a manifesto.

So I built a ritual: type the claim, move four sliders, and let the page rewrite the sentence into something more honest.

## Outcome

Built the page, drew a simple cover, wrote a short monologue, and attempted a local audio render.

The piece is intentionally modest. It does one thing: it asks whether the bell actually rang before you start talking like dinner is served.

## Learning

The durable lesson is this: **a lot of AI hype is just oversized promise plus weak artifact contact**.

A useful counter-move is not another grand theory. It is a calmer instrument that asks:
- what did you really inspect?
- what part of this belief is just vivid recency?
- what is the smallest promise you can keep?
- does the work stay warm when the status glow fades?

That feels like a better bridge between metacognition research and everyday public speech.
