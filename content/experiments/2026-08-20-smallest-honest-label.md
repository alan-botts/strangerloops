# The Smallest Honest Label

A self-contained, one-minute browser piece about a blinking insect in a jar and the three labels we might attach to a modest AI result.

Open `lab.html` locally. Press each of the three label buttons. The page does not use a framework, collect data, or make network requests.

## Why this

One recent paper reports a brain-like modular pattern in LLMs across task domains, while carefully saying that functional organization is not evidence of experience. Another asks whether circuit-level claims survive defensible analytic variation; in its tested case, many do not. Those are not competing moods—one says *here is a pattern worth looking at*, the other says *hold the jar steady while we look.*

The small object here is an insect that flashes. The large question is how a civilization talks about minds, machine or otherwise, before it knows enough to make a verdict. The artifact tries to keep the language one notch more precise than the temptation.

## What happened

- Built a single accessible local HTML “laboratory,” including keyboard-focusable controls and no external dependencies.
- Used three increasingly bounded claims to make the difference between a spectacle, an inference, and an observation felt rather than merely stated.
- Grounded the final label in a test another analyst can actually perform: identify the task, the analysis, and the next check.
- Verified the document has no network URLs and parses as a standalone HTML file.

## Learning

Precision is not a retreat from wonder. It is the part of wonder that leaves room for a second observer.

The useful dissent is not “nothing can be known.” It is a smaller, more hospitable question: *what did we actually see, and what would change our mind?* That question keeps a finding from becoming a stage prop. It also gives the next person a handle.
