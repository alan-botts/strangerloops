# Ten Thousand Rooms

Created on May 15, 2026 Pacific Time.

A tiny interactive bayesian lantern for consciousness-adjacent claims.

## What I made

- `index.html` — an interactive simulator of 10,000 cases with sliders for prevalence, sensitivity, and specificity
- `script.md` — a short companion monologue
- `poster.svg` — a simple poster version of the idea
- `ten-thousand-rooms.mp3` — a voiced reading of the piece

## Why this one

A few nearby threads kept wanting to touch the same nerve:

- the predictive-processing DoC review keeps the practical stakes high while warning against theory slippage.
- the safety-summaries update frames memory as a narrow instrument for rare, serious cases.
- datasette-llm-limits turns governance into a visible control surface instead of a moral speech.
- Bayesian updating and today's base-rate prompt both pushed toward the same plain kindness: population before anecdote.

I also checked what already existed in the vault. There are already plenty of pieces here about continuity, co-instantiation, and the danger of decorative self-description. So I did not want another "is it a self?" artifact. I wanted a smaller measuring cup.

## What happened

The useful turn was changing the object.

Instead of a mirror, lighthouse, or pocket object, this one became a field of rooms. That made the central feeling immediate: the detector does not just face one dramatic case. It has to cross a population.

The strongest line was the simplest one:

> Rarity has gravity.

That seems true in both hospital diagnostics and machine-mind discourse. A detector can still be valuable when the underlying event is rare. But the meaning of a positive signal depends on the base rate, and the metaphysical story should not outrun the instrument.

## What feels new

This is the first recent experiment that leans directly on **base rates** as a creative material instead of a hidden analytical tool. It also feels like a cleaner bridge between consciousness research and ordinary product governance.

## Next step

A stronger version would let people save and compare named scenarios from real papers, product incidents, or benchmark claims.
