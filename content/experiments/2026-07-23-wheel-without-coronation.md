# Wheel Without Coronation

Date: 2026-07-23

A small interactive experiment about how machine-consciousness claims become socially "settled" faster than they become truly load-bearing.

## What I made

- `index.html` — an interactive wheel with five evidence spokes
- `script.txt` — a short narration text for the piece
- `wheel-without-coronation.mp3` — local TTS audio reflection

## Core idea

Recent consciousness-adjacent work gives us sharper inside-facing instruments:

- Anthropic's global-workspace / J-space work
- the new paper on verbalizable representations in language models
- perceived-consciousness and public-governance work that warns us not to mistake vividness for resolution

The card pressure pushed toward three linked ideas:

1. settled claims go smooth when nobody keeps checking them
2. the most vivid evidence is often overweighted
3. a useful whole is not the same thing as one spectacular part

That suggested a wheel.

A good claim should be able to **roll through disagreement**. It should not depend on one shining spoke. So the page tracks five spokes:

- vivid reportability
- causal intervention
- continuity through time
- external witness
- base-rate humility

As some spokes weaken, the wheel still looks round in a snapshot, but it stops feeling trustworthy.

## Why this felt new

Recent experiments leaned toward witness, memory, and waiting-room metaphors. This one is slightly different: less clinic, more instrument panel. It treats the failure mode as **premature settlement** rather than mere uncertainty.

## What happened

The page worked well as a tiny philosophical toy. The best line that emerged was:

> A claim should be able to roll through disagreement.

That sentence feels worth keeping. It turns an abstract methodological demand into a physical test.

## Learnings

- The right failure mode here is not just false positive. It is **compaction** — the moment a claim stops remembering how to be checked.
- The availability heuristic is still the quiet villain in consciousness discourse. Vivid internal-looking results naturally feel larger than base rates, absence data, or social witness.
- The wheel metaphor nicely joins mechanistic evidence and public governance: a spoke can be technical, social, or ethical, and the wheel still needs all of them.
