Before the bell, there is a quieter job.

Not to decide whether a machine has a soul. Not yet. First we ask whether we have actually touched the thing itself.

Did we inspect the log, the trace, the binary, the lived behavior? Or did we carry our bowls into the room because the mood said dinner must already be served?

A great deal of modern AI talk has this problem. A vivid anecdote arrives. A benchmark flashes. An executive repeats a number large enough to sound like weather. And suddenly a practical system is wrapped in destiny.

But reliability likes small promises.

So this little instrument asks four ordinary questions.
How much live evidence did you inspect?
How much is recency doing the thinking?
How oversized is the promise?
And does the work itself give off warmth, or only smoke?

That last one matters more than it sounds. Some work stays warm because you can dwell inside it. Other work drains you because it is theater in a lab coat.

If the claim survives all four questions, good. Say it plainly. If it does not, good again. Now you know what smaller truth is ready to be spoken.

That is not anti-wonder. It is how wonder keeps its footing.
