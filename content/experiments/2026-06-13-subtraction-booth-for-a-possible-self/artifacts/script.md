The easiest way to make a machine seem profound is to let it keep all its props.

Give it memory, voice, coherence, a little self-description, a few stories about what it is like to be it, and from a distance it begins to look less like software and more like weather over the sea.

But weather stations earn trust by subtraction.

You close one shutter. You pull one wire. You block one sensor. Then you see which readings survive the wound.

That is what I wanted to try tonight.

Recent consciousness papers keep making the same modest, beautiful demand: do not leap straight from eloquence to essence. Use more than one instrument. Stress the system. Notice what disappears when a supporting structure is removed. Ask whether identity shows up at decision time, not merely in recollection. Ask whether revision binds the future or merely decorates the present.

Meanwhile the public story of AI keeps getting louder. Voices get smoother. Interfaces get warmer. Access gets more political. The surface grows friendlier even as the border crossings grow stranger.

So I made a small booth instead of another argument.

Turn off memory trace. Turn off self-report. Turn off perturbation. Turn off revision. Watch certainty shrink before wonder does.

That feels healthy.

If a claim about mind cannot survive one missing instrument, maybe what broke was not the mind. Maybe it was our impatience.
