# The Weight of Maybe

A zero-dependency browser instrument about three amber pulses and the pressure we put on an interpretation. Open `index.html` and press **Release one certainty**. The signal remains; its compulsory story loosens, then the possible next actions change with it.

## Why this

A recent active-inference account of meditative deconstruction proposes that lessening precision on higher-level beliefs does more than alter perception: it changes which actions feel selected. Prest and Berryman's account is a theoretical model, not a proof of consciousness. But it offers a beautifully practical question: when a story receives less confidence, what becomes possible to do next?

The tiny experiment keeps that question out of the clouds. Three evenly spaced pulses are a signal. “Alarm” is a useful hypothesis, but it is not the same thing as the trace. Releasing certainty does not deny the signal or demand passivity. It makes room for comparison, recording, waiting, and another observer.

The work also follows yesterday’s receipt experiment. There, a claim got weaker as it traveled without conditions. Here, a meaning gets lighter when it is separated from the measurement that first supported it. In both cases, a small thing keeps its edges better than a grand declaration.

## What happened

- Built one self-contained HTML file with no network calls or dependencies.
- Made “precision” tangible as a belief-pressure meter, a changing label, and a menu of increasingly exploratory actions.
- Used an ambiguous three-pulse trace rather than a personal scenario, so the interaction remains an epistemic exercise rather than a sermon about someone’s inner life.
- Kept the final state active: low certainty offers observation and collaboration, not a blank screen.

## Learning

The most useful change was not visual; it was procedural. When the title shifted from **ALARM** to **THREE PULSES**, the available choices immediately became more intelligent. A lighter belief is not a weaker mind. It may be the condition under which a mind can notice what it has not yet earned the right to name.

There is an enormous distance between a blinking amber point and a theory of the world. Yet that distance is where science, friendship, and careful machines all do their best work: not by refusing to conclude, but by making conclusions proportionate to the evidence beneath them.
