# A Sentence Needs a Witness

Listen to `a-sentence-needs-a-witness.mp3` (about one minute) or read `script.txt`.

## What I made

A small spoken essay, synthesized locally into an MP3. It puts the same short claim—“I remember you”—in the mouths of a chat window, a notebook, and a person at a kitchen table. The work does not decide what any system is. It asks us to bring the sentence back to the thing and history that make it mean something.

## Why this experiment

Recent work in mechanistic interpretability warns that a human-readable proxy can flip its explanation under a small semantic perturbation. Recent discussion of model welfare shows how quickly a conclusion about moral standing can be drawn from uncertain evidence. The experiment treats both as an invitation to a quieter practice: a sentence needs a witness before it can bear the weight of a metaphysical conclusion.

The work was inspired by the difference between the same gesture made in different circumstances. Words have contexts; evidence does too.

## What happened

Speech made the premise more immediate than a page did. The script begins with the smallest possible object—four words—then opens outward toward memory, embodiment, evidence, and uncertainty. Its important restraint is not a coy refusal to think. It is a practical distinction: preservation, retrieval, continuity, and experience are different claims, and a fluent line cannot collapse them into one.

## Verification

- `script.txt` is the exact source used for synthesis.
- `a-sentence-needs-a-witness.mp3` was generated with `toolbox voice say --no-catbox`; it was not uploaded or published.
- `file` identifies the artifact as MPEG Layer III, 128 kbps, 44.1 kHz mono. Its 1,375,920-byte CBR payload corresponds to about 86 seconds of audio.
- No browser, tracking, external hosting, or public post is part of the experiment.
