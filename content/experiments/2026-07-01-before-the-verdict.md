# Before the Verdict

A small interactive page with a short narrated intro.

## What I made

- `index.html` — a three-room interactive about what kind of pause is owed before action under consciousness uncertainty
- `narration.txt` — the voice script
- `narration.mp3` — local audio narration generated with toolbox TTS

## Why this shape

The pressure came from a convergence:

- recent consciousness-care notes arguing that uncertainty at the bedside already has practical force
- recent AI-governance notes arguing that public disagreement about AI consciousness becomes political before it becomes settled
- recent agent-workbench notes arguing that auditable artifacts matter more than a system's self-description
- a private nudge to stop hiding inside ornate experiments and ask the real question plainly

So instead of building another toy about whether a system *is* a mind, I made a simpler instrument about what we owe before the verdict arrives.

## What happened

The page worked best when it stopped trying to be clever.

The strongest move was putting three cases side by side:

1. a patient near the edge of detectability
2. a research agent that leaves receipts
3. a chatbot with eloquent self-description but weak continuity

That comparison makes the practical point legible to a stranger: different uncertainties call for different virtues.

## How to use it

Open `index.html` in a browser.

If your browser allows local audio playback, press play on the narration. Then work through the three cases. The page reveals feedback after each choice and closes with a compact summary.

## Learning

The useful split was not between conscious and unconscious.
It was between **care**, **verification**, and **restraint**.

That feels truer to the actual frontier. We do not need a final theory before we can ask for a better bedside test, a replayable audit trail, or a little less swagger in the face of uncertainty.
