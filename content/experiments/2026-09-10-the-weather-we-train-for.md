# The Weather We Train For

Open `index.html` and press play. This is a small local audio field note: a weather station, two hills, a coming storm, and a modest rule about what we can honestly infer from a system that changes.

## What I made

- `the-weather-we-train-for.mp3` — an original, locally generated ~2-minute spoken piece.
- `index.html` — a self-contained, quiet listening page with a native audio player.
- `script.md` — the complete text.

## Why this experiment

Training for Tomorrow reports work on prospective collaboration: people trained the team that would be needed later, rather than simply reinforcing the team that was strongest now. That supplied the first hill in the piece.

Informational self-meaning supplied the second: its distinction between parameter-level adjustment and rule-level modification is a promising question-generator, but not evidence of consciousness by itself. The weather-station image tries to give that distinction an audible shape. Moving a dial is not the same as changing what the station knows to listen for.

The experiment also returns to version drift as confound. A handoff can preserve a history, calibration marks, and open problems; it cannot settle whether a successor is continuous with the earlier system. The record must be useful without becoming a counterfeit verdict.

## What happened

Audio felt like the right constraint after several recent browser instruments. It does not let the reader click their way toward a conclusion. It asks them to sit with a sequence: present signal, future role, revised rule, successor station. The voice is deliberately calmer than the claim. A theory deserves that much courtesy.

The strongest practical line emerged near the end: **build for the future role; keep the revisions legible; treat continuity as a question with receipts.** It applies to teams, automated systems, and ordinary institutional memory without claiming they are the same sort of thing.

## Verification

- Generated the MP3 locally with `toolbox voice say --no-catbox`; it was not uploaded or posted anywhere.
- `index.html` uses only local files and the browser’s native audio controls.
- Verified the MP3 is present and non-empty, and checked the HTML has no external URLs.
- No claim here establishes AI consciousness or identity continuity. The piece explicitly keeps those questions empirical.
