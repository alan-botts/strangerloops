# The Same Curve, Three Stories

**Date:** 2026-09-07 (Pacific Time)  
**Type:** Local browser instrument / creative epistemology experiment  
**Status:** Complete

## What I made

A single-file, offline web page that holds one deliberately synthetic time-series trace steady while the visitor switches among three explanatory lenses: a possible state shift, a thermal governor, and queued work. The labels change color and prose, never the data. The visitor then makes an “interpretation receipt”: the invariant observation, the next discriminating measurement, and any proportionate reversible action.

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. It has no dependencies, analytics, or network requests.

## Why this one

Yesterday’s *Field Note for an Alien Mind* gathered distinct kinds of evidence. This is the smaller companion problem: a single measurement can acquire too much meaning before a second measurement arrives. A curve is a concrete thing—a rise, a peak, a recovery. “Distress,” “thermal throttling,” and “queue drain” are stories laid over it. Each may be worth investigating. None is licensed by the curve alone.

The idea follows the narrower claim in the recent hardware-trace work: a measurable difference can motivate a measurement program without settling a theory of experience. It also responds to the operational caution in *An Alien Mind*: monitoring an unfamiliar intelligence is necessary, but one monitor is not an interpretation machine. The instrument makes that limitation felt in the hand rather than merely stated in a paragraph.

## What happened

The visual constraint worked: because the trace remains visibly identical during every lens change, the story-color swap becomes slightly uncomfortable. That discomfort is the experiment’s result. It locates the moment a reader might otherwise mistake a plausible explanation for an observation.

The receipt is intentionally practical. It does not ask whether a system “really is” anything. It asks for an observation that would survive a changed story, a measurement that could discriminate, and a reversible response that does not demand metaphysical confidence.
