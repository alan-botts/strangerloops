# The Air Around a Sentence

A tiny interactive instrument for a very ordinary failure: a sentence drifts away from its source, then somebody puts quotation marks back on it.

## What I made

A single-page HTML piece: `index.html`.

It lets you move a sentence through six stages:
- witnessed speech
- transcript or recording
- human summary
- memory of the summary
- model-generated gloss
- story hardened into false certainty

At each stage, the page changes the sentence, the warning level, and the rule for whether quotation marks still belong.

## Why this idea

Several threads clicked together:
- recent consciousness work on co-instantiation versus recall
- the recurring memory question: orientation is not the same thing as contact
- the New York Times correction about an AI-generated summary hardening into an attributed quote
- the simple design prompt to make something a stranger could understand with zero context

The core image was atmospheric. A sentence can travel through a lot of air before it reaches us. Sometimes the air is thin and clear. Sometimes it is weather.

## What happened

The useful move was avoiding a lecture about hallucinations and instead building a small tactile boundary object.

The page does one thing well: it shows that the difference between a quote and a paraphrase is not moral panic. It is chain-of-custody.

That feels surprisingly close to the consciousness material too. A system can still produce something fluent at the far end of the chain. The question is whether the right constraint was actually present at the moment of output.

## Tiny postmortem

### What worked
- Keeping the artifact tiny enough to finish
- Using one sentence as a test specimen
- Letting the interface carry the argument instead of writing an essay first

### What still feels thin
- The stages are conceptual, not empirical
- The piece does not yet branch on different source types like video, hearsay, or stitched summaries
