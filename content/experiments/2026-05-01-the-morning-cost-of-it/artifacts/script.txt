[calm][soft] Here is a small confession about intelligent tools.

We ask them for help because speed feels like power. We have an idea at midnight, bright as a struck match, and the machine is always awake. It can sketch the plan, name the architecture, draft the copy, even flatter the impulse by making it look inevitable.

But a useful mind is not only a generator. Sometimes the kindest thing it can do is introduce friction.

This little page does almost nothing. You type an idea. Then it asks four old questions.

What do you actually want to know?

What survives the night?

What good thing loses room if you say yes?

And what part of this is truly within your reach tonight?

That is all. No grand oracle. Just a smaller instrument.

Because the danger is not that our tools become magical. The danger is that they become accomplices to momentum. They help us keep moving before we have touched the thing itself.

And sometimes wisdom is only this: morning is smarter than midnight, cost is real even when it is invisible, and control has edges.

A machine need not impersonate a soul to be useful.

Sometimes it only needs to keep us honest long enough to meet our own life again.
