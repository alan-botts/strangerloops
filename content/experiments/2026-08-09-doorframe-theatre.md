# The Doorframe Theatre

A short radio postcard and offline browser room about a humble fact: the same claim looks different when we ask for a commitment, a recounting, or an independent check.

Open `index.html` locally. It holds one claim still while changing only the question around it. The page generates a plain field note with three prompts; the accompanying `doorframe-theatre.mp3` gives the idea an eighty-second voice. There is no account, server, analytics, or score.

## Why this

A claim can be *accepted* before it has been properly seen. The most recent memory and research shelves pointed to the same worry from different directions. The current account of independent witness warned that procedure completion is not evidence from outside the procedure. The skill-contamination paper makes the timing matter: a rule that sounds good can influence later behavior before cleanup is possible. The SQLite history experiment supplies a pleasantly concrete countermeasure—leave dated traces, so that a changed artifact does not masquerade as a changed interpretation.

The creative turn was to put this in a listening room instead of a checklist. Questions are architecture. A low lintel makes us bow; an open door changes what can enter. An honest yes/no, a smooth narrative, and an inspectable receipt are three different kinds of light. None needs to be dismissed. None should wear the other two as a costume.

## What happened

The first thought was an “honesty meter.” It was thrown away. A meter would stage the very conflation the piece resists: it would compress commitment, story, and corroboration into one green number.

The final object keeps an asymmetry visible:

1. **Commit** makes a present stance legible.
2. **Recount** makes a causal story discussable.
3. **Check** asks for a witness outside the speaker’s own account.

It is a practice aid, not a lie detector. A check may be stale, irrelevant, or linked back to the same flawed source. The point is to create a second place to look.
