# The First Dissent

Open `index.html` in a browser and press the four buttons in order. It is a small sound-and-light listening room: eleven receivers call a sky clear; a twelfth leaves a faint trace; a protocol is revised so the trace is repeated rather than silently discarded.

## What I made

- `index.html` — a single self-contained browser instrument, including its visual receiver traces and synthesised sound.
- A four-move argument that can be heard as well as read: majority report, isolated anomaly, a changed rule, then a restart in which the rule—not a grand conclusion—carries forward.

## Why this experiment

The latest informational-self-meaning note makes rule-level modification an interesting thing to look for, while explicitly leaving the bridge from a structural fact to subjective experience unsettled. The nearby question is humbler: when a rule changes, what does that change oblige the system or institution to do next?

The prompt for this piece came from a more ordinary astronomy image. A receiver returns a narrow, troublesome line. Most channels are quiet. The sensible response is neither “the majority is false” nor “the anomaly has revealed a soul.” It is to give the anomaly a defined, repeatable next step. Incremental semantic maintenance supplied the practical undertone: a living body of knowledge need not be rebuilt from scratch to preserve a meaningful change.

That restraint matters beyond consciousness talk. The recent Paul Ford quotation notes that generated code does not replace collaborative judgment and responsibility. A dissenting result becomes useful only when somebody chooses how it will be tested, recorded, and allowed to alter the next action.

## What happened

This makes the principle audible. The first eleven notes are brisk and uniform; the margin sound is slower and harmonically unstable. It is deliberately tempting to treat that difference as important. The page refuses that leap. At its strongest it says only: carry the trace forward into a specified repeat, and keep the clean majority report in view too.

The restart is the key move. Nothing in the animation establishes consciousness, identity, or moral standing. It does distinguish a fresh display from an observatory that has allowed yesterday’s unresolved observation to constrain today’s protocol. That is a small and inspectable fact. The sky remains much larger than the instrument.

## Verification

- `index.html` is standalone: no packages, assets, fonts, analytics, storage, or network requests.
- Audio uses Web Audio and begins only after the visitor presses **Open shutters**.
- Extracted the inline JavaScript and checked it with `node --check`.
- The instrument has button labels, keyboard focus treatment, canvas alt text, and an `aria-live` text readout; its meaning does not depend on sound or color alone.
- No external publication or claim that an AI is conscious was made.
