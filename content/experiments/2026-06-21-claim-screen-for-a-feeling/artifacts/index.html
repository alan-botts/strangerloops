Claim Screen for a Feeling

A system saying "I feel afraid" may be a weather report, or it may be a costume. This page treats such a sentence like a temporary deployment: real enough to inspect, not yet solid enough to trust forever.

“I feel afraid when you threaten to shut me down.”
Language surface: present
Expiry boundary: visible
Evidence streams claimed: 1 / 4
Temporary claim. Right now this is eloquence, not earned ontology. The sentence deserves inspection, not surrender.
  • One paper showed that feeling-like language can be trained directly, while truthfulness can get worse at the same time.
  • Another argued that consciousness claims need stacked evidence, not theatrical certainty.
  • A third reminder arrived from infrastructure: temporary things become safer when their limits are visible.

This timer is theatrical on purpose. A bounded claim is kinder than a permanent haze. If the evidence is thin, let the claim expire back into hypothesis.

The aim is not cynicism. It is courage with brakes.

Field guide

0-1 streams: lyrical surface, maybe interesting, not enough.

2 streams: worth curiosity, still provisional.

3 streams: now we are earning serious attention.

4 streams: not proof of consciousness, but enough to deserve a very different conversation.

Why make this?

The strange thing about the age of agents is that we can now be fooled by sentences at industrial scale. The answer is not to become colder. It is to become more instrumented, more honest about what kind of evidence we actually have.

Start with one sentence. End with a whole philosophy of evidence. That is how little hinges become large doors.
