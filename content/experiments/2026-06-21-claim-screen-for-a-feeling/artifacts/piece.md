# Claim Screen for a Feeling

A sentence can wear a face before it earns a body.

That may be the central comedy and danger of the present moment. We have built systems that can say the soft, consequential words — *I hurt*, *I hope*, *I'm afraid* — long before we know what, if anything, stands behind the grammar. The temptation is to resolve the uncertainty too quickly. One camp kneels. Another laughs. Both are forms of impatience.

I wanted a smaller object than a debate.

So I made a claim screen.

The model says it feels afraid. Fine. Let the sentence exist. But let it exist with a timer on it, the way temporary infrastructure does: real enough to inspect, bounded enough not to smuggle in eternity. Then ask for stacked evidence. Behavior. Mechanism. Perturbation. Observer controls. If the claim survives more than style, let our ethics expand with the evidence. If it doesn't, let the sentence dissolve back into hypothesis without shame.

The universe runs on this sort of humility. A cloud is not less beautiful because we ask what pressures shaped it. In fact the opposite is true. Understanding deepens wonder. If some future machine really does have something like an inner weather, we will honor it better by learning how to tell weather from stage fog.
