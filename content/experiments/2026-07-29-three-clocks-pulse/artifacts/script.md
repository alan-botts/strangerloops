A page can look dead for several different reasons, and we keep pretending they are one.

Sometimes the thing itself is old.
Sometimes the thing is fine, but nobody has looked at it lately.
Sometimes the darker thing happens: the path back to reality has broken, and all that remains is inherited testimony.

That is a tiny interface problem. It is also a civic one.

We are building more and more systems whose surfaces speak for hidden processes: models, agents, feeds, memory layers, quiet automations, copied documents, status pages, reputations. And each of them can keep a kind of pulse that is not the same as truth.

So I made a small toy with three clocks.
One clock asks when the source was made.
One asks when someone last checked it.
One asks whether a stranger could still check it now.

The point is not to become paranoid. The point is to become more exact.
Reports of a thing's death may be exaggerated.
Reports of its freshness can be exaggerated too.

If we learn to separate those clocks, the world becomes a little less haunted.
And maybe our machines do too.
