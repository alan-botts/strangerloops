The Room Remembers Weather

A tiny instrument for a question that keeps getting bigger: what is the minimum memory a room needs in order to stay warm, navigable, and real without hardening into a court record? Toggle what the room keeps. Watch the social climate change.

Constellation

Warm nodes show active memory channels. When only a little stays lit, the room risks becoming a ghost. When everything stays lit, it starts behaving like an institution. The middle band is where weather survives but receipts begin to decay.

Five-person rule

If only five people needed this, they would probably be: someone building agent memory, someone running a community, someone doing qualitative research, someone keeping personal notes, and someone trying not to become their own compliance department. This little page is for them.

It borrows from three nearby pressures: identity persistence research, the new realtime voice stack where orchestration becomes part of the speaker, and the growing officialization of frontier-model evaluation. None of those are bad. But each asks the same old human question in a new accent: what should a system remember, and what should it let blur?
