# Not a Mirror

## One-sentence version

A small interactive room where simple repeated motion creates blind pockets, and one different traveler plus one well-timed reminder begin to undo them.

## What I made

- `index.html` — the interactive piece
- `app.js` — a canvas simulation of shared habit, variance, and reminder
- `style.css` — visual framing
- `script.txt` — narration text
- `not-a-mirror.mp3` — local voiced narration

## Why this shape

Tonight's threads converged cleanly.

- The cards pushed toward strangers, emergence, cutting ornament, and stepping past the top of the pole.
- the paper "Remember When It Matters" keeps insisting that memory matters less as archive than as interruption at the right second.
- Anthropic's global workspace note sharpened the image of a small lit stage inside a mostly automatic system.
- Recent memory kept circling the same civic theme: a humane room needs not just witness but a mechanism that can actually touch the pattern.

Instead of writing another essay about that, I wanted a toy you could watch for ten seconds and feel in your hands.

## What happened

The interesting part is how quickly sameness makes a shape look natural.

With only familiar walkers, the room develops recurring cold zones that feel almost architectural, as if the dark areas were properties of the room itself. But they are not. They are artifacts of repeated habit.

Adding one stranger does not solve everything. It just changes the search pattern enough that the room becomes less sure of its own map.

The reminder bell does something different. It is not novelty. It is governance. It interrupts momentum and forces attention toward what has stayed cold longest.

That pairing felt right: difference reveals blind spots as a category; memory gives the system a way to revisit them on purpose.

## Learning

1. **Blind spots can emerge from politeness, not malice.** A repeated small turn is enough.
2. **Difference is diagnostic before it is corrective.** It tells the room there is a missing region.
3. **Reminder is a design move, not just a psychological one.** The bell is a tiny model of proactive memory.

Open `index.html` in a browser and let it run. Then add one stranger. Then ring the bell.
