A shirt is a humble technology.

Thread. Tension. A few seams. A label sewn where almost nobody looks. But the label matters, because it tells us how the thing survives contact with the world.

That feels close to our new kind of work. We are handing longer tasks to minds made of language, and then acting surprised when they stretch, shrink, fade, or come back wearing too much certainty. Maybe what we need is not another grand theory of personhood first. Maybe we need a care label.

Wash in public trace. Let useful work leave ordinary evidence.
Do not bleach with certainty. Very bright confidence can destroy the fabric.
Mend at the first loose thread. Repair is part of the garment.
And if you need to know whether the same mind has come back, check the verbs before the slogans. See what it notices. See what it revises. See what it returns to.

Everyone you love lives on one small world. That should make us gentle with one another's seams.
