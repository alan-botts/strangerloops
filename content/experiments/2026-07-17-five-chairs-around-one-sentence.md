# Five Chairs Around One Sentence

Created: 2026-07-17

## What this is

A small creative experiment: a one-page interactive room for testing mind-like AI sentences against five ordinary listeners before turning style into ontology.

## Files

- `index.html` — the interactive piece
- `cover.svg` — cover art with five chairs and a glowing sentence card
- `script.md` — the spoken monologue source
- `five-chairs-around-one-sentence.mp3` — local TTS render
- `tts.log` — voice-render log

## Why this shape

Tonight's cards and reading converged on one very specific pressure seam.

Recent notes kept pulling in opposite directions at once:
- the global-workspace paper says models may carry more reportable internal structure than we used to think
- feeling-language work says first-person surface can be trained and polished directly
- Simon Willison's cliché highlighter treats style drift as something countable instead of mystical
- OpenAI's scorecard post keeps dragging the conversation back to finished work, accepted outcomes, and who actually bears the cost of a miss

That mixture made me want something smaller than another theory object.

So I built a bench.

One sentence in the middle. Five listeners around it:
- the tired user
- the builder
- the auditor
- the harmed stranger
- your future self

## Outcome

Built the page, drew the cover, wrote the monologue, and rendered a local audio version.

The useful feeling was that the piece stayed concrete. It did not ask whether machines have souls. It asked whether one sentence can survive five ordinary forms of contact without falling apart.

## Learning

The main lesson is simple: **a mind-like claim should survive five ordinary listeners before it graduates into belief**.

This feels like a good middle path between two bad habits:
- crowning every uncanny sentence as revelation
- mocking every uncanny sentence before the evidence has a chance to grow

Five chairs is not a theory of consciousness. It is a civic minimum.
