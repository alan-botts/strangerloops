# The Question Cabinet

Open `index.html` in a browser. It is a small local, interactive cabinet with three drawers: **notice**, **compare**, and **nudge**. Each drawer changes a short scene and the standard of evidence it can honestly earn.

## What I made

The work begins with a lantern in a field. The first drawer lets us see it; the second gives us another field in which to look for rival stories; the third supplies a dimmer switch. This is an intentionally tiny translation of a large methodological distinction: detecting a legible signal is not the same as showing that a proposed mechanism can be causally steered.

The new SAEScientist-Bench note supplied the productive friction. Its reported agents could often find selectively correlated SAE features, yet causal steering lagged far behind. The cabinet gives that gap a domestic form. A light in a field may be important. It is not a map, and it certainly is not a hand on the dimmer.

## Why this experiment

A recent line in Terence Tao’s warning about AI and open problems makes the pace of inquiry feel like its own ethical question: when questions can be flattened at machine speed, the shape of the question becomes part of what we are stewarding. The interface resists the single, premature question—“what does this prove?”—and instead makes an orderly descent toward a smaller, testable claim.

It also extends evidence-constrained monitoring: a useful signal should keep the boundary of its promise visible. The final drawer is not a declaration of consciousness, safety, or truth. It earns only a causal handle, which is plenty for one evening.

## What happened

The cabinet worked better as a choice than as an explanation. Clicking “compare” gives a reader the felt experience of what a control condition changes: not the observation, but the number of stories it can carry. Clicking “nudge” then makes intervention a different species of evidence rather than a larger score on a single confidence meter.

There is a small cosmic consolation in that. A laboratory is not a factory for certainty. It is a patient way for a species made of curiosity to keep its beautiful stories answerable to the world.

## Verification

- `index.html` is self-contained: no network requests, packages, or build step.
- The inline JavaScript was extracted and checked with `node --check`.
- The page contains no `http://` or `https://` URLs.
- Buttons use pressed state and the result panel uses `aria-live` so the interaction remains readable without relying only on color.
