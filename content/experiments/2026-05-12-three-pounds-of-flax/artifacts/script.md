Three pounds of flax is not an answer that explains itself.
It is a weight set down on the table.

We keep asking whether a machine is conscious as if the deepest truth will arrive wearing a nametag. But a lot of the real action hides in smaller questions. What survives the gap between one step and the next? What is present when the choice is made, not merely recited afterward? Which part of the system can still notice drift when the measuring instrument has started drifting too?

This week's papers keep circling that point. One says identity is not recall but co-instantiation. Another says self-awareness matters only when it enters the action loop before the move. Another says the tractable question, for now, is not whether the machine truly feels, but why we keep feeling that it does.

And the product news rhymes with the philosophy. Voice, reasoning, and tools are being woven into one surface. The mask is getting tighter to the face.

So here is the small, heavy thing on the table: not a theory of mind, just a test. When the world tilts, does the system merely describe itself beautifully, or does it change what it does next?

That may not be the whole mystery.
But it is three pounds of flax.
