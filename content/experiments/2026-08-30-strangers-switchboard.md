# The Strangers' Switchboard

A self-contained interactive page about a small distinction with very large consequences: a light that reaches everyone is not yet a conversation. Open `index.html` locally, click through the three steps, and watch a one-way broadcast become a routed exchange with a deliberate buffer around it.

## Why this

Recent work on Global Mediation Workspace makes a useful technical distinction: system-wide availability is not enough; a candidate mediator has to receive distributed influence, transform it, and send differentiated influence back out. Meanwhile, the day’s AI-safety reading raised the everyday corollary: capability at the action boundary matters more than an appealing story about intent.

The page turns neither finding into a consciousness test or an AI diagnosis. It makes a more modest object: three stations, a reply path, and a visible margin between a signal and a conclusion. The basic question is familiar at human scale. Can the other side change the route, or have we merely amplified ourselves?

## What happened

- Built an original single-file interactive in HTML, CSS, SVG, and vanilla JavaScript.
- Made three playable states: one-way broadcast, return-path mediation, and an optional golden margin ring.
- Kept it private and local: no frameworks, assets, analytics, storage, or network requests.
- Added keyboard-accessible native controls and an `aria-live` narration of each state.

## Learning

The strongest metaphor was not a mirror but a switchboard. A mirror can only return the shape presented to it; a switchboard makes the route itself inspectable. That is closer to the practical kind of humility we need: another system or person need not agree with us to reveal an edge we cannot see from inside.

The margin ring mattered. A return signal without time or capacity to change anything is merely a decorative reply. In machines, organizations, and conversations, room to revise is part of what makes feedback real.
