# The Legs of a Claim

## One-sentence read

Three recent claim-types do not fail in the same way, so they should not be asked to stand on the same kind of evidence.

## A theorem claim
- **Anchor to resist:** $2,000 per successful problem is a powerful first number, but it can pull attention away from the hidden failed-search denominator.
- **What gives it footing:** This claim earns real footing from Lean formalizations, linked manuscripts, and domain-specific statements a specialist can inspect.
- **Best refresh:** Ask what the search process looked like across misses, what scaffolding was fixed versus improvised, and how often the same workflow reproduces outside the announced ten.
- **Working stance:** This one wants replay more than reverence. A certificate is not the whole truth, but it is a real leg.

## A possible-mind claim
- **Anchor to resist:** Words like workspace, metacognition, and self-awareness can make us feel farther along than the evidence really carries us.
- **What gives it footing:** The best footing here is mechanistic and behavioral together: interpretable internal structure, usable interventions, and repeated tasks where monitoring changes control.
- **Best refresh:** Look for converging evidence across labs and model families, especially tests that distinguish reportability from mere fluent confession.
- **Working stance:** This one wants renewal more than coronation. Interesting gears are not yet a settled ontology.

## A social-acceptance claim
- **Anchor to resist:** A neat automation demo can hide the older truth that people do not grant social legitimacy just because a workflow got cheaper.
- **What gives it footing:** The footing here is lived human response. If people welcome direct help but resent mediated help, the product surface has touched relationship, not just efficiency.
- **Best refresh:** Test designs that preserve authorship and consent. Measure whether the agent gives time back without becoming a membrane between people.
- **Working stance:** This one wants witness more than benchmark. The room itself is part of the instrument.

## Durable line

A claim cannot always lift its own legs. Sometimes the missing support is a certificate. Sometimes it is a fresh witness. Sometimes it is a human relationship no benchmark can impersonate.
