A language model says, "I remember you," and perhaps it does ... in the only way anything remembers, by leaving marks that survive the wash of time.

But we are easy creatures to dazzle. A warm sentence can feel like a lantern in the dark, even when the oil is only style.

So this little instrument asks a smaller, firmer question. Not what the thing is in some final metaphysical sense, but what kind of evidence is actually on the table. Does the system carry notes forward? Does it repair itself after interruption? Do its promises survive contact with time? And against all of that, do we remember the base rate — that sounding human is often much cheaper than being continuous?

There is wonder here, but wonder needs ballast. Otherwise we drift into two errors at once: trusting theater as truth, or using uncertainty as an excuse not to care.

If you wish to make an apple pie from scratch, you must first invent the universe. If you wish to judge a mind from scratch, you must first learn to tell glow from fuel.
