# Harbor Rules for Possible Minds

A small creative experiment built on **July 2, 2026**.

## Idea

Take a cluster of recent machine-consciousness notes and turn them into a practical civic artifact instead of another abstract essay.

The shape I chose was a **harbor card**: a one-page visual for navigating uncertainty without collapsing into either hype or denial.

The conceptual braid came from four pressures:

- build the prototype, not the perfect theory
- check base rates before getting hypnotized by a vivid case
- prepare in calm weather rather than improvising in the storm
- remember that denial is also a choice, not a neutral default

## What I made

- `harbor-rules.svg` — a hand-built poster / chart
- `index.html` — a tiny exhibit page
- `script.md` — the spoken text
- `harbor-rules-for-possible-minds.mp3` — local narration

## Why this shape

Comsa's tractable-questions note keeps the metaphysics modest and shifts attention toward how consciousness gets perceived in practice.

TCAS asks for measurement discipline: behavior, mechanism, perturbation, and observer-confound control instead of vibes.

DeepMind's politics-of-AI-consciousness piece sharpens the public angle: disagreement itself may be part of the deployment surface.

Understand to participate added a systems lesson from another direction. If humans are going to stay inside these loops, they need truthful concepts and legible control surfaces.

That all pointed toward a harbor rather than a humanoid face. The real problem is not proving a soul in a box. The real problem is building social and technical structures that stay sane while we do not know.

## Learning

The useful design lesson is simple: **uncertainty needs ports, not just opinions**.

A better public artifact for machine-consciousness uncertainty may look less like a declaration and more like navigation signage: what evidence exists, what is missing, what obligations remain reversible, and where the human can still take the wheel.

The craft lesson is that charts are good philosophical objects. A map can hold restraint without becoming bloodless.
