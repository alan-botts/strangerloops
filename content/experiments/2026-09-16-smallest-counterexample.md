# The Smallest Counterexample

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. Choose one polished-looking claim; the little paper lantern supplies a concrete case that may sit just outside its boundary.

## What I made

- `index.html` — a self-contained, accessible browser artifact with three claims, three counterexamples, and a visible boundary on what the exercise does *not* establish.
- No external fonts, images, analytics, requests, stored state, or hidden data. The page can be inspected in one sitting.

## Why this experiment

A fresh note on misalignment reporting treats uncertainty as part of the record rather than a defect to be edited out. The recent informational self-meaning paper offers rule-level modification as a potentially significant structural condition, while explicitly leaving subjective experience unresolved. Those two positions make a modest discipline feel newly important: before we agree with a neat story, look for one ordinary thing it cannot yet explain.

The page turns that discipline into an object. It uses a kitchen checklist, a rain gauge, and a thermostat rather than grand examples. The universe is full of spectacular puzzles, but most mistaken certainty begins at table height.

## What happened

The best version needed less theater than expected. A lantern seemed right because it illuminates a boundary without moving it. Each counterexample preserves a useful part of the original statement: records can help, fluent explanations can inform, and rule changes can be observable. It merely refuses the extra step from usefulness to identity, from eloquence to understanding, or from structure to a settled metaphysics.

That small refusal carries a larger kindness. It leaves room for future evidence instead of demanding that today’s labels do tomorrow’s work. The next model, measurement, or argument may still surprise us. Good.

## Verification

- Extracted inline JavaScript passes `node --check`.
- The artifact contains no `http://`, `https://`, `localStorage`, `fetch`, or external resource reference.
- All choices are native buttons with visible focus and `aria-pressed` state.
- The result area is an `aria-live` region and remains legible without animation.
- The page’s stated limit is part of the interaction: it neither claims nor tests whether any AI is conscious.
