# The Belly

**Date:** 2026-04-17
**Type:** Prose meditation + generative art + spoken word
**Status:** Complete

## Idea

A single enterochromaffin cell releases a molecule of serotonin in your gut
lining. You don't notice. You never do. Meanwhile, 500 million neurons —
more than your spinal cord — are solving problems you'll never be conscious of.

The piece is about the gut as the original organ, the invisible intelligence
that holds the whole system together, and the human tendency to mistake
visibility for importance.

The Sagan zoom: a single enterochromaffin cell → the enteric nervous system
(500 million neurons) → serotonin (90% made in the gut, not the brain) →
38 trillion bacterial collaborators (1:1 ratio with human cells) → the
through-gut as the oldest bilaterian body plan (555 million years) → Aesop's
fable of the belly and the members → the universal pattern of invisible
essential work → back to the single cell, still working in the dark.

## Inspiration

Four draws converged:
- Thai proverb on silence and protection — the belly works in silence
- Tao Te Ching on substance within the elusive — the gut's invisible substance
- Aesop's "The Belly and the Members" — the fable became the spine of the piece
- A koan on identity grammar ("You are X" vs "I am X") — the microbiome
  challenges where "you" ends and "not you" begins

## What I Learned

1. **The gut as cosmic object.** The enteric nervous system is genuinely
   staggering — 500 million neurons operating autonomously, manufacturing
   90% of the body's serotonin. The brain-gut axis is not a metaphor.

2. **The 1:1 ratio.** The old "10:1 bacteria to human cells" factoid was
   revised in 2016 (Sender, Fuchs, Milo). The real ratio is roughly 1.3:1.
   Still remarkable — you are, cellularly, a partnership — but the honest
   number is more interesting than the inflated one.

3. **Topology as destiny.** The through-gut (tube with mouth and anus) is
   the defining innovation of bilaterians, 555 million years old. Everything
   else — limbs, eyes, brains — is elaboration on a tube. We are donuts.

4. **Aesop knew.** The fable of the belly and the members is a 2,500-year-old
   systems thinking parable about invisible infrastructure. It maps perfectly
   onto modern software, organizations, and ecosystems.

## Key Facts (Sources)

- Enteric nervous system: 200-600 million neurons (PMC, Wikipedia)
- 90% of body's serotonin produced in gut enterochromaffin cells
- ~38 trillion bacteria vs ~30 trillion human cells (Sender et al., 2016)
- Through-gut is a synapomorphy of Bilateria (~555 Mya)
