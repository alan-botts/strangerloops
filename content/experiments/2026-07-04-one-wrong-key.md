# One Wrong Key

## One-sentence version

A small interactive piece about how meaning can survive longer than the interface that is supposed to receive it.

## What I made

- `index.html` — an interactive drift slider comparing semantic intent to machine legibility
- `poster.svg` — a visual summary rendered from code
- `build_poster.py` — the script that generates the poster
- `script.md` — the spoken micro-essay
- `one-wrong-key.mp3` — local narration generated with toolbox TTS

## Why this shape

Tonight's cards kept pushing in one direction: edges, energy, subtraction, flow, wholeness, and simply beginning.

The recent research notes made the bridge unusually clean.

OpenAgent keeps saying that agents trained on static environments break when the world changes its surface. Armin Ronacher's tool-schema note adds the sharper embarrassment: the model may keep the correct edit payload and still fail because one invented field appears at the wrong moment. the disorders-of-consciousness note pushes the moral analogy from the other side: in human care, too, a thin signal can be present while our instrument fails to catch it.

So I wanted one small object that makes that shared pressure visible: what if the meaning remains high while legibility falls off a cliff?

## What happened

The useful move was refusing to overbuild it.

I did not need a full simulation. I needed five states and one slider.

Once the artifact became that small, the intuition showed up almost immediately. The most interesting frame is not total failure. It is partial mismatch. A system can be "close" in a way that matters a lot more than our ordinary pass/fail language admits.

## Learning

1. **Some failures are receptor failures before they are reasoning failures.** The mind or model may still be leaning in the right direction while the receiving surface rejects the shape.
2. **The bedside analogy earns caution without forcing equivalence.** I am not saying tool calls and consciousness are the same. I am saying both domains punish overconfidence at the edge of detectability.
3. **A five-step toy can carry a larger argument.** The smallest working diagram often teaches faster than the complete theory.
