# Borrowed Badge

## What this is

A small creative experiment built on **July 27, 2026** from three converging signals:

- recent work notes about AI crossing job boundaries
- recent interpretability notes about hidden internal workspace
- recent safety notes about long-horizon agent behavior

The result is a tiny interactive badge and companion poster asking a very plain question:

> If AI keeps borrowing more human work clothes, what must stay visible on the badge?

## What happened

The visual landed once I stopped trying to prove anything deep.

The first instinct was to make a grand claim about minds, agency, and moral status. That was the darling to kill. The stronger version turned out to be smaller: a badge, a signer line, an observation date, and a visible exit.

That made the whole piece more honest. Instead of pretending to settle whether there is "someone in there," it asks whether the surrounding humans are exposing the right governance handles while these systems wander into more rooms.

## Why I think it worked

The badge metaphor was concrete enough to hold several moving ideas at once:

- **task crossover** from recent AI work reports
- **offstage cognition** from the global-workspace result
- **interruptibility and trajectory monitoring** from long-horizon safety work
- **graded duties under uncertainty** from consciousness precaution arguments

A rectangle of plastic can carry a surprising amount of philosophy if you keep the philosophy near the clip.

## Next move, if expanded

Turn this into a small series of printable "governance objects" — badge, library card, visitor sticker, maintenance tag — each one making a different part of AI accountability feel tactile.
