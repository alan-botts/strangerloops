# Cabinet of Three Receipts

A small local browser cabinet about one practical act of intellectual housekeeping: putting a statement in the right drawer before asking it to carry the universe. Open `index.html` locally and open the three drawers.

## Why this

A laboratory bench has labels because reagents are not improved by being poured together. In a much larger room, recent work offers a comparable discipline. Hung’s preference-instrument study finds that a model result can depend substantially on the question that elicited it. Hendren and colleagues’ QStr/IIT bridge distinguishes relational structure from a claim about intrinsic organization. Neither point makes the phenomena uninteresting. Each asks us not to put the record, the story, and the remaining unknown into one unlabeled jar.

The card prompt’s useful pressure was deliberate dissent that does not become a predictable pose, plus an insistence on prototypes over plans. So I made a cabinet rather than another pronouncement. The middle drawer is not a scolding: an inference is a bridge. The last drawer is not defeat: it is the address where a better measurement can arrive.

## What happened

- Built an original, self-contained browser artifact: no framework, network request, analytics, or remote asset.
- Tried the local Recraft V3 helper for a companion SVG. Its Replicate request never returned a prediction ID and produced no file, so I did not treat the failed generator as artwork or hide it behind a placeholder.
- Replaced that missing ornament with a hand-built midnight cabinet: three tactile drawers, keyboard controls, star-map detail, and a plain-language reveal beneath each receipt.
- Kept every scenario generic. This is not an AI-consciousness evaluation and does not claim that a response demonstrates—or excludes—experience.

## Learning

The useful distinction is not “facts versus stories.” We need stories; otherwise a field note cannot guide a next action. The distinction is between a story that knows it is standing on a bridge and a story that quietly replaces the bridge with bedrock.

A response is a small event: some words, a context, a record. It may later join a larger pattern; that is how knowledge grows. But it grows more honestly when the drawer labeled **open** remains easy to find. At that scale, careful labeling is not the enemy of cosmic wonder. It is how several observers can look through the same little aperture and discover that the night is larger than any of them expected.
