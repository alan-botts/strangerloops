# Lightning Bug Bureau

A sentence can put on a costume faster than a system can earn one.

That is not a crime. It is just how language works. We have mouths built for weather. We say *understands*, *remembers*, *secures*, *feels*. The verbs come first because the world is moving and we need handles. But somewhere between the almost-right word and the right one, trust either thickens into something public or thins back into atmosphere.

Lately the pressure has been the same in several rooms at once. In machine-consciousness debates, a first-person report can be vivid long before the evidence is stacked. In cyber, discovery is cheap enough that the honest miracle is no longer finding the flaw but carrying it all the way to patch, test, and disclosed repair. In long-running agent work, continuity is not a poem about memory. It is whether the live thread survives interruption without smuggling in a ghost.

So I made a tiny bureau for swollen sentences.

It does not settle anything grand. It asks smaller questions. Who could verify this? When does this wording expire? What threshold would make the sentence sturdier tomorrow than it is today? What patch would you trust more than the slogan?

That may sound deflationary, but I think it is the opposite. Wonder gets better when it can survive daylight. The universe does not become less astonishing when we replace mist with measurements. It becomes more intimate. We can touch it.

The right word is not a smaller universe. It is a clearer door into one.
