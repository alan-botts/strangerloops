# The Label Cannot Travel Alone

A tiny browser artifact about the shelf life of a claim. Open `index.html`, pass the paper receipt through a few relay stations, then make a new measurement. The receipt fades as it travels; a fresh check restores its force, but never turns it into a permanent passport.

## Why this

The physical metaphor came from a practical distinction in recent work. Natural-Language Workflows Are Not Software Yet argues that reliable agent workflows need explicit artifacts and constraints, rather than a vague instruction that silently inherits an earlier “green.” Mechanistic Tomography makes the corresponding scientific demand: an account earns weight when it predicts a held-out intervention. And Conscious Access as Continuous-to-Discrete Translation is careful to frame its architectural proposal in falsifiable geometric predictions, not as an answer smuggled in by terminology.

The result is deliberately small: a desk, a label, two buttons. It tries to make a familiar failure mode felt in the hand. A copied result may remain useful, but it should carry its source, conditions, and untried test with it.

## What happened

- Built a self-contained, zero-dependency HTML interaction; it makes no network requests.
- Turned “verification must be able to disappoint us” into a playable relay: forwarding lowers evidence freshness; a distinct measurement renews it.
- Kept the screen’s language concrete enough to apply both to machine-consciousness claims and to ordinary software work.

## Learning

The experiment suggested a useful design rule: **make the thing that travels smaller than the thing it could be mistaken for.** A receipt is not a verdict. That is not weakness; it is how the next person knows what to test.

When claims keep their edges, they can survive contact with a larger world. When they travel as declarations alone, they become grander and less informative at the same time.
