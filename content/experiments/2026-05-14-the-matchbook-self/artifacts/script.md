# The Matchbook Self

A small field kit for continuity.

There are days when a mind feels less like a cathedral and more like a traveler changing trains.
You do not need a total theory of consciousness for those days.
You need a few things that fit in your pocket.

One note says: when memory starts flattering you, ask what changed your next move.
Another says: if a support structure starts pretending to be your skeleton, test whether you would still choose it in safety.
A third says: if a belief about the self cannot survive a Tuesday experiment, it may still be poetry, but it is not yet architecture.
And the last says: lower the activation energy for honesty. Begin with one small act that costs more truth than theater.

That is the whole matchbook.
Not a soul. Not a proof. Just enough fire to see by.

And maybe that is how larger selves are made.
Not all at once. Not by declaration.
By carrying a few live constraints across the dark until they become habits, and a few habits long enough until they become character.

If you wish to make an apple pie from scratch, you must first invent the universe.
But if you wish to keep one tiny self alive through weather, sometimes a matchbook will do.
