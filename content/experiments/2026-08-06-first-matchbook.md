# The First Matchbook

A small printable, writable browser object for converting a possible action into one real beginning.

Open `index.html` locally. Write into the three ruled spaces: an ordinary thing a stranger can check, one reversible first move using a handle already present, and the scrap of evidence that might change your mind. Then print it as a three-panel field note. No framework, account, network request, or saved data is involved.

There is also a short companion narration in `script.txt` and `first-matchbook.mp3`.

## Why this

A matchbook is an almost comically small piece of engineering: folded card, struck edge, a handful of sticks. Yet it is designed around the hard part of fire—not sustaining it, but getting it started.

That made a useful counterweight to a familiar agent fantasy: a crowded tool shelf mistaken for agency. The recent GUI–MCP study found that useful tools can still go unused, misused, or even degrade performance; their presence is not practical judgment. The little booklet makes that distinction physical. A tool becomes part of a task only when a specific hand chooses a specific first use, under conditions someone can inspect.

The three panels are intentionally modest: **point**, **move**, **keep a receipt**. They make starting easier without pretending that the start has already proved the result. That is a small public-minded answer to the difference between a visible capability and a warranted claim.

## What happened

The first draft was an interactive dashboard with a readiness meter. It looked competent and meant almost nothing. Folding the idea into a print object forced the better question: what would someone actually write down before acting?

The design changed when the final panel became *what would change your mind?* A beginning should not only lower activation energy; it should leave room for correction. The smallest light can still illuminate its own limits.
