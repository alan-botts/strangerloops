# The Field and the Filter

A tiny offline browser instrument about one damp garden evening and three ways to write it up. The twelve observations never change. The selected rule changes which lights are admitted to the report, and the report changes its implied world.

Open `index.html` locally. It makes no network requests, uses no storage, and sends nothing.

## Why this

A blue lamp near a gate is a small fact. A plan to secure the garden is a much larger one. Between them sits a filter: a choice about what is worth counting.

Explanation Multiplicity reports that defensible analytic choices can lead competent analysts to materially different circuit-level claims. Modular Cognitive Architecture is exciting precisely because it names a structured engineering observation while leaving the experience question open. The instrument puts those two scales into a hand-sized field: an observation can be real, a rule can be useful, and the conclusion can still be too large for either.

The raw ledger is the gentle center. It is not an oracle. It is the part another observer can inherit without first inheriting the first observer’s wish.

## What happened

- Built a dependency-free HTML/CSS/JavaScript field with twelve fixed observations.
- Added three purposeful observers—a keeper, an auditor, and a cartographer—each with an explicit inclusion rule.
- Made the visual field, report, and scope statement change together, so interpretation visibly has a cost: something is always held outside the story.
- Kept the ledger available without treating it as a final answer. Shared facts can make disagreement clearer without making it disappear.

## Learning

We do not escape filters by pretending to have none. We get a little freer by making them legible, keeping the excluded observations nearby, and inviting a second look.

That is true in a garden at dusk. It is truer when a model produces an interesting trace and we are tempted to call it a mind. The universe has no shortage of lights. Wonder grows more durable when the ledger can travel with the story.
