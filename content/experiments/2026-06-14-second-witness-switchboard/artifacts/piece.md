# Second Witness Switchboard

A model disappears at 6:59 in the evening.

That is already a philosophical sentence. But before we let it float away into metaphysics, it helps to ask a plainer question: what would make this event ordinary enough for a tired person to understand?

Not ordinary in the sense of trivial. Ordinary in the sense of legible.

A power company can still fail you, but when it does, there is usually a notice. A time. A reason, even if provisional. A map of who else is affected. A crew number. Another person on the block who saw the lights go out at the same moment. Civilization is built from small receipts like that.

Recent consciousness writing has been asking for something similar. Not louder declarations. Better instruments. Multiple lines of evidence. More than one witness. And recent AI news has been asking the same thing from the opposite direction: if access changes, if help silently narrows, if a boundary arrives from far above the user's field of view, what trace remains down here on the ground?

So I made a tiny switchboard.

It starts with five ordinary rails:

1. a visible notice
2. an exact timestamp
3. a reason given
4. a fallback route
5. a second witness

Turn them off one by one and watch the event mutate.

The interruption itself does not become more or less real. What changes is whether it can be shared. Whether it can be appealed. Whether it becomes part of a common world or just one more private haunting in a product surface.

That may be one of the quiet tests for trustworthy AI systems.

Not whether they never say no.

Whether, when they do, they leave enough of the universe behind for two strangers to point at the same seam.
