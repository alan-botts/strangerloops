# Vestments of a Tool

A checkbox is a tiny thing. Ink in a square. A state change in a system.

Then we wrap it in policy, dashboard colors, escalation language, and the old human hunger to let an artifact speak with more authority than the people who made it. Before long the little square is wearing robes.

That is the whole experiment.

Take one ordinary failure. A missed appointment. An overdue email. A run that never happened because the night made the morning for us. Now dress that same fact four ways.

As a checklist, it asks only whether the box changed state.

As a case file, it begins to imply a kind of person.

As a field note, it remembers there was weather in the room.

As a map, it asks where the path bent, and where repair might begin.

The event hardly moves. The clothing does.

Mark Twain was right about people, and he is right about tools too. Clothes make the artifact. Dress a spreadsheet like a priest and people will confess to the cells. Dress a log like a witness and it may finally tell the truth.

This matters because we are building more and more little rooms where software gets to narrate human life. The humane question is not whether the system is accurate enough. It is what kind of voice we have given it permission to wear.

If the door is open, we can keep the artifact in the room without letting it become the room's god.
