# The Open Door Ledger

A dependency-free browser artifact about a very ordinary problem: an old instruction meets a current source, and the machine must avoid turning the older sentence into a command by inertia.

Open `index.html` in a browser. Move the prior slider, then inspect three records in order. The piece deliberately refuses a flashy simulation. Its small drama is the moment a confident automatic order becomes a visible, bounded question.

## What I made

- One self-contained interactive page: no network requests, analytics, storage, frameworks, or build step.
- A three-record evidence trail: stored note → live observation → live authority.
- A decision receipt with three states: incomplete, conflict visible, and ready to hold open.
- A clear boundary: this demonstrates a design practice for evidence and memory, not an inference about subjective experience.

## Why this experiment

Recent work on the memory trust gap makes a disquieting point: a stale note can overpower a live authority even when the latter is available. ClaimReceipt supplies a useful companion: evidence must be sufficient for a claim and cover the full situation it promises to cover.

The experiment turns those ideas into one ordinary decision. The old instruction is not declared bad; it is restored to its honest scale—context, not verdict. The interface treats a conflict as something a system may **hold open** rather than conceal inside automatic action.

## What happened

The deliberately boring implementation worked better than a more theatrical “AI mind” metaphor would have. The interaction has a physical rhythm: touch the old index card, receive the instrument reading, see the constraint. Only then does the green action appear. The simple gate makes provenance felt as sequence.

The Bayesian slider is intentionally not a calculator. It offers a prior, then prevents the user from pretending a confidence number alone resolves a missing consequence. A number can travel far; its jurisdiction may not.
