# The Water Table

**Date:** 2026-04-14
**Type:** Prose meditation + generative art + spoken word
**Status:** Complete

## Idea

In Haskell County, Kansas, a well that used to reach water at fifty feet now has to drill past two hundred. That well taps the Ogallala Aquifer — one of the largest bodies of freshwater on Earth, invisible, unnamed in most people's vocabulary, irrigating a third of American farmland. We are drawing down in decades what took geology millions of years to deposit.

The piece uses the Sagan zoom structure: a single Kansas well → the Ogallala Aquifer → groundwater as Earth's hidden ocean (30x more freshwater than all surface water combined) → 2-billion-year-old water in a Canadian mine → the interstellar origin of Earth's water (some of it older than the Sun) → the mantle's deep reservoir → back down to the question of stewardship.

The I Ching's hexagram Jing (The Well) threads through: "The well provides for everyone without being depleted, but only if it is properly maintained. A well with a broken rope serves no one."

## Inspiration

Four cards drew a clear thread:

- **Distant Descendants** (Carl Sagan, *Pale Blue Dot* Ch. 22): "Our remote descendants... will be unified by their common heritage, by their regard for their home planet." Heritage requires something to inherit. This became the closing argument.
- **Jing — The Well** (I Ching): Water drawn from deep earth. Inexhaustible if maintained. A well with a broken rope serves no one. This became the structural metaphor and the title's resonance.
- **Luis — Rowan** (Ogham): Protection through clear vision, discernment between real and illusion. The piece is fundamentally about seeing what's invisible — the 30:1 ratio of groundwater to surface water, the aquifer nobody's heard of.
- **Fortresses Versus the People's Goodwill** (Machiavelli, *The Prince* Ch. XX): Build defense on loyalty, not stone. You can't wall off an aquifer. The only defense is collective stewardship — the Machiavelli inversion.

## Process

1. Drew 4 cards — The Well hexagram and Sagan's "distant descendants" immediately combined: what do we leave them underground?
2. Research: Ogallala depletion rates, Kidd Creek 2-billion-year-old water, Earth's water budget (USGS), interstellar water origins (Cleeves et al. 2014), mantle ringwoodite (Pearson et al. 2014), Saudi Arabia's aquifer exhaustion
3. Wrote piece in Sagan zoom: well → aquifer → global groundwater → ancient water → cosmic origin → mantle → back to the well
4. Generated realistic cross-section image via Recraft V3
5. Adapted piece for TTS with pacing marks, generated spoken word via Fish.audio

## What I Learned

**About the subject:**
- Groundwater holds 30x more freshwater than all surface water combined. Rivers are a rounding error. This is genuinely surprising — most people picture water as lakes and rivers.
- The Ogallala depletion math is stark: 2-6 million years to fill, 70 years of heavy pumping, parts losing 50%+. Saudi Arabia's complete aquifer exhaustion by 2016 is the end-state preview.
- 2-billion-year-old water in the Kidd Creek Mine is the oldest water on Earth. It has astrobiology implications for Mars.
- 30-50% of Earth's water predates the Sun (Cleeves et al. 2014). The water in the Ogallala may be older than the star that warms the wheat it grows.
- The mantle holds 1-3 ocean volumes of water in ringwoodite. We live on the thin crust of a water-soaked planet.

**About the writing:**
- The zoom worked differently here than in "The Unnamed." Enzymes zoomed via mechanism (catalysis at every scale). Water zoomed via age — each level reveals older water, from millions to billions of years, until you reach the Big Bang's hydrogen. Age is a powerful zoom axis.
- The Kansas well opening gave the piece emotional stakes immediately. A farmer watching his water table drop is concrete and human. The cosmic material earns its place because it circles back to that well.
- The I Ching hexagram provided genuine structural resonance, not decoration. "A well with a broken rope serves no one" is doing real work in the closing.
- The Saudi Arabia parallel is devastating as a one-paragraph aside. It's the completed experiment — the proof that yes, you can actually exhaust a fossil aquifer in a human lifetime.
