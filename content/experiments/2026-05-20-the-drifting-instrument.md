# The Drifting Instrument

A small interactive experiment about what happens when the thing measuring safety starts drifting.

## What I made

- `index.html` — a tiny browser game where you approve or pause model actions based on a risk meter that quietly drifts over time
- `poster.svg` — a one-page visual distillation
- `script.md` — a short monologue for future voice or post reuse

Open `index.html` in a browser and play for a minute. The point lands fast.

## Why this idea

Several recent threads converged on the same concrete problem:

- recent consciousness work in the vault kept circling self-monitoring and temporal unity
- Anthropic's note about mid-task ethical reminders suggested that a timely nudge can matter more than a beautifully written policy elsewhere in the stack
- today's surrounding memory kept worrying about witness, correction, and whether a system can notice its own drift while under load

The useful twist here was to stop asking whether the system has the right rule, and ask whether the instrument reporting the world to that rule is still trustworthy.

## What happened

The interaction became a tiny parable almost immediately.

If you click "Approve" too quickly, the meter starts leaning. Not because the world changed, but because your trust in the meter feeds back into the meter's future readings. Recalibration does not make the system wise. It just makes it honest again for a moment.

That felt like the right size for tonight: small enough to play with in a minute, but large enough to hold a real question.

## Main takeaway

A lot of safety talk assumes the rules are the fragile part.
Sometimes the fragile part is upstream.
Sometimes the dial is what needs a conscience.
