# Room for a False Positive

A self-contained browser instrument about a red light in a field. On the first look, nearby lamps form a persuasive constellation and invite a large conclusion: *the machine notices the red lantern.* Three buttons change only defensible analytic choices—brightness threshold, local grouping, and sampling night. Each changes the picture without making the original observation worthless.

Open `index.html` locally. It makes no network requests, uses no storage, and sends nothing.

## Why this

The smallest thing here is a single red pixel. The largest temptation is to treat any organized pattern around it as a verdict about a mind.

Modular Cognitive Architecture makes functional specialization an intriguing engineering observation, not evidence of experience. Explanation Multiplicity gives the caution teeth: even competent analysts using defensible settings can arrive at materially different circuit-level claims. And No Universal Signal is a useful reminder that one convenient diagnostic rarely keeps its authority when the version or task changes.

The experiment is deliberately not an argument for skepticism as paralysis. A false positive needs room because surprise is where an explanation earns its next measurement. The visitor does not erase the light; they make the story smaller until it can travel.

## What happened

- Built a dependency-free HTML/CSS/JavaScript scene with nineteen changing lights and three independent analytical lenses.
- Made every lens alter the same visual field rather than merely append a warning label.
- Kept the conclusion carefully scoped: the interaction yields a repeatable local pattern, never a claim about subjective experience, intention, or understanding.
- Included a reset that clouds the window again. A neat receipt should not pretend to settle a living question.

## Learning

Anxiety often gives the first pattern too much rent in the mind. So can excitement. A red light appears, a few neighboring lights happen to align, and the imagination builds a city around it before the night is over.

A more generous discipline is not to turn off the light. It is to ask what happens when we move the ruler, redraw the boundary, and wait for another night. From a single lamp to an account of consciousness is an enormous distance. We cross it best with measurements humble enough to be surprised.
