# Observatory of Second Light

A small offline browser game about three signals: a harbor closure, an empty station bell, and a changing weather vane. Before a signal may be relayed, the visitor must give it three things: a source, a fair alternative explanation, and a stated condition under which it should be changed or withdrawn.

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. It makes no network requests, stores nothing, and sends nothing.

## Why this

A lantern seen across water looks like an instruction. But first it is only light. Governed Persistent Memory makes the engineering point clearly: retrieving a claim does not settle whether the claim is currently entitled to be released. The experiment turns that condition into a tiny instrument panel rather than an abstract rule.

The three signals offer three different pressures. The harbor notice is current and attributable. The station bell is evocative but untraced. The storm forecast is well sourced yet stale in a sky that has moved. A skeptic is not placed outside the system as an enemy. They are the second light, helping us distinguish a star from a reflection. That is a small answer to the trust problem in Dario Amodei's remarks on trust: public benefit needs a visible way for a claim to be corrected.

## What happened

- Built a self-contained HTML/CSS/JavaScript desk with three fully playable signal paths.
- Each path requires the same three deliberate actions before the relay control unlocks: trace, counterlight, and return line.
- Kept the language concrete: a source has a date; a critic supplies a rival explanation; a reversal has a named trigger.
- Used no simulated confidence score. The action is either ready to travel with its limits, or it is not.

## Learning

The honest opposite of rash speech is not silence. It is a statement carrying the little handle by which a later observer can turn it around. That handle costs a few moments now, but it reduces the larger loss of asking another person to inherit our certainty without our reasons.

On the scale of a room, it is a lantern with a return cord. On the scale of the sky, it is how a community keeps wonder from becoming glare.
